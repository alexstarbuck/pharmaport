"Interventni uvoz" is industry shorthand; the statute doesn't use the term. The legal basis is **ZoL čl. 129(1)**: HALMED *"može izvanredno dati suglasnost za unošenje ili uvoz lijeka za koji nije dano odobrenje za stavljanje u promet u Republici Hrvatskoj"* (may exceptionally consent to bringing in or importing a medicine without a Croatian marketing authorisation). Listed grounds include medically justified need, research, clinical trials, natural disasters and emergencies, and individual treatment prescribed by a doctor on their own responsibility. Detailed conditions are left to a ministerial regulation (čl. 129(2)). **That regulation was apparently never published.** A search of every NN issue 2013–2026, HALMED's list of regulations and the legal bases of all 17 medicines regulations found nothing. Yet ZoL čl. 228(3) still refers to *"pravilniku iz članka 129. stavka 2."* The old NN 197/03 regulation (under the repealed 2003 Act) is labelled "na snazi" on zakon.hr but is not in the transitional list of ZoL čl. 243 → treated as not applicable; flagged. **So in practice the procedure rests on the Act plus HALMED's page.**

HALMED's own name for it confirms the term: questions go to **interventniuvoz@halmed.hr** (HALMED page *Suglasnost za unošenje i uvoz lijekova*, fetched 2026-10-09, `01_sources/halmed/suglasnosti.txt`).

---
## The finding that limits the plan

HALMED's page splits the two routes by **who may apply**:

- **Bringing in from the EU/EEA (*unošenje*)** — application by *"veleprodaje lijekova koje djelatnost prometa lijekovima na veliko obavljaju sukladno članku 115."* → a licensed wholesaler. **Pharmaport could do this once licensed.**
- **Import from third countries (*uvoz*)** — application by *"uvoznik (nositelj proizvodne dozvole koja obuhvaća uvoz lijekova iz trećih zemalja)"* → only a holder of a **manufacturing licence** covering import. **A wholesale licence alone doesn't qualify.**

Consistent with ZoL čl. 3 t. 91, which defines *uvoz lijeka* as wholesale of medicines from third countries.

**Risk, not a blocker:** the court register lists *"djelatnost uvoza lijeka"* as a registered activity. That's only a registered activity, not a permission. Third-country import would need a separate manufacturing/import licence — a much bigger undertaking. Treat *interventni uvoz* as **EU/EEA *unošenje* only** unless that changes.

## What each consent application needs (per HALMED page — not the regulation itself)

- Written request; data entered in HALMED's "Lijekovi" program, on CD/DVD
- Either a reasoned statement from a healthcare institution (not a pharmacy) signed by its head and its medicines-committee chair, **or** a doctor's prescription (original + copy; max. 150 prescriptions per program entry)
- A proforma invoice tracing the full supply route; the manufacturer responsible for batch release
- Proof of payment of procedure costs
- One consent **per supplier**; valid until the quantities are delivered, max. 1 year
- Immunologicals and blood/plasma products: batch data + OCABR certificate

Preconditions include: authorised in an EU member state (or a third country if not available in the EU); labelled in an EU language in Latin script; no Croatian-authorised alternative; urgent, medically justified need.

## Further obligations found in the Act (verified 2026-10-09)
- **Recall/suspension:** for a medicine on the market under a čl. 129 consent, the procedure *"pokreće veleprodaja koja je u Republici Hrvatskoj stavila u promet navedeni lijek"* (is started by the wholesaler that put it on the market) — ZoL čl. 62(7).
- **Reporting:** ZoL čl. 186(8) (NN 136/25): wholesalers must regularly report ordered and delivered quantities for extraordinary entry/import under čl. 129(1) indents 1, 6 and 7 to HALMED and the Ministry, via the shortage system.
- **Penalty:** ZoL čl. 227(1) t. 15 — bringing in/importing a medicine *"bez propisane suglasnosti"* (without the required consent); range per the agent report 9.290–13.270 € (*range not re-checked by me*).
